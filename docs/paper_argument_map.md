# Paper argument map

1. **Background:** Information acquired before a collective decision can benefit all participants.
2. **Problem:** Acquisition is privately costly even when its value is shared.
3. **Strategic tension:** Each agent can prefer that the other pays for research.
4. **Model:** Two agents independently choose Research or Skip before a collective decision. One Research action yields full shared value \(V\); each researcher pays \(c\).
5. **Economic mechanism:** Shared benefit plus private cost gives a free-riding incentive and asymmetric pure equilibria for \(0<c<V\).
6. **Computational contribution:** A reproducible NashPy model, independent pure best-response checker, and cost sweep verify the formal benchmark.
7. **Behavioral gap:** Equilibrium calculations do not establish real AI or human choices.
8. **Competing explanations to test:** (A) strategic free-riding with correct beliefs; (B) bounded reasoning or inaccurate beliefs about the other's Research choice.
9. **Current PS1 contribution:** The current contribution is a formal and reproducible baseline for studying costly information acquisition before collective AI decisions, together with a clearly testable behavioral gap.

**Evidence boundary:** No real AI, human, or learning-effect observation has been made.
